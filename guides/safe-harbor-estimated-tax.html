The Safe Harbor Rule for Estimated Tax, Explained

Estimating your taxes perfectly is impossible when your income changes month to month. The good news: the IRS doesn't require perfection. It offers safe harbors — payment targets that protect you from the underpayment penalty even if you end up owing more when you file.

What is the underpayment penalty?

If you don't pay enough tax through withholding and estimated payments by each due date, you may owe a penalty — even if you're due a refund when you file (IRS: Underpayment of estimated tax by individuals penalty). It's calculated like interest: the underpaid amount, times the IRS underpayment rate, times the number of days it was late, figured separately for each installment.

The safe harbors

You generally won't owe the penalty if any of these is true (IRS penalty page; Form 1040-ES):

  1. You owe less than $1,000 after subtracting withholding and refundable credits.
  2. You paid at least 90% of the tax for the current year (2026), on time.
  3. You paid at least 100% of the tax shown on your prior-year return (2025), on time — provided that return covered all 12 months.

Your required annual payment is the smaller of options 2 and 3. Divide it by four and pay that amount by each due date.

The 110% rule for higher earners

If your prior-year adjusted gross income was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately), option 3 becomes 110% of last year's tax (Publication 505).

Which safe harbor should you use?

  • Income going up this year? The prior-year safe harbor is usually easiest and cheapest: it's a known number (line 24, "total tax," of your 2025 Form 1040) and it doesn't move no matter how well this year goes. You'll still owe the remaining balance in April — so keep saving.
  • Income going down this year? The 90% current-year target may be lower. It requires a reasonable forecast, and you need to update it as the year goes on.

Examples

Illustrations only, not real filings.

Rising income. Jordan's 2025 total tax was $8,000 and AGI was $70,000. In 2026 his freelance business doubles and his real tax will be about $18,000. Prior-year safe harbor: $8,000, or $2,000 per quarter. If he pays $2,000 on each of the four dates, he owes no underpayment penalty — he'll pay the remaining ~$10,000 with his return.

High earner. Priya's 2025 AGI was $210,000 and total tax $45,000. Her safe harbor is 110% × $45,000 = $49,500, or $12,375 per installment — unless 90% of her 2026 tax is lower.

Falling income. Sam paid $20,000 of tax in 2025 but expects only $9,000 for 2026. 90% of $9,000 = $8,100, which is far lower than $20,000, so the current-year target is the better choice.

First-year freelancers

If your total tax for 2025 was zero and you were a U.S. citizen or resident for the full year, you owe no estimated tax penalty for 2026 (Form 1040-ES). You'll still owe the full tax when you file, so save for it.

If you had a W-2 job in 2025, you did have a liability — your safe harbor is 100% (or 110%) of that tax, and withholding from your old job counts toward it.

Behind for 2026? What to do before January 15, 2027

  • Pay missed installments now. The penalty grows daily; paying stops it from growing on that installment. Payments are applied to the earliest underpaid installment first.
  • Increase W-2 withholding if you have a job: withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year, so extra withholding in November and December can cover earlier shortfalls (Publication 505).
  • Consider the annualized income installment method (Form 2210, Schedule AI) if most of your income came late in the year.
  • File early: you can skip the Jan 15 payment if you file by February 1, 2027 and pay in full.

FAQ

Does the safe harbor mean I don't owe the tax? No. It only removes the penalty. Any balance is still due by the April filing deadline.

Do state taxes have safe harbors? Many do, with their own rules. Check your state revenue department.

Is a refund last year the same as zero liability? No. Look at line 24 (total tax), not your refund.
